# Research Status

## Current Working Hypothesis

A materially significant number of Muslim immigrants and refugees may have entered Ottoman Palestine during the 19th and early 20th centuries, including Circassians, Bosnians, Albanians, Egyptians, and migrants from neighboring Ottoman provinces.

## Current Evidence Position

The evidence confirms organized Ottoman refugee settlement in Palestine, especially Circassian settlements in northern Palestine. It does not yet prove very large-scale immigration into Palestine proper.

## Current Directly Identified Settlements

### Kfar Kama

- Group: Circassian
- Approximate founding: 1878
- Reported population: approx. 1,150 settlers
- Confidence: Medium pending primary Ottoman confirmation

### Rehaniya

- Group: Circassian
- Approximate founding: late 1870s
- Reported population: approx. 35 families, estimated 175-250 people
- Confidence: Medium pending primary Ottoman confirmation

### Gâbe / Gabiya

- Group: Circassian
- Reported 1884 population: 369
- Possible initial settlement: 1,950, unverified
- Confidence: Low until Ottoman source is confirmed

## Running Totals

### Documented / Reported Caucasian Muslim Settlement in Palestine Proper

- Conservative: approx. 1,700-1,800
- Aggressive pending confirmation: approx. 3,300

### Documented Arab Muslim Immigration

- Directly documented count so far: 0

This does not mean no Arab migration occurred. It means no quantified direct Ottoman record has yet been extracted.

### Demographic Scenario Estimates

Possible external Arab/Muslim immigration into Palestine, 1880-1914:

- Low: 5,000-15,000
- Medium: 15,000-25,000
- Aggressive but not yet proven: 25,000-50,000

These are scenario estimates, not documented counts.
