# Status Update: Frantzman Dissertation Processing

## Source Processed

Seth J. Frantzman, *The Arab Settlement of Late Ottoman and Mandatory Palestine: New Village Formation and Settlement Fixation, 1871-1948*, PhD dissertation, Hebrew University of Jerusalem, 2010.

## Why This Source Matters

This dissertation is one of the most directly relevant sources found so far because it systematically studies new Arab and Muslim village formation in Palestine from 1871 to 1948, using maps, censuses, aerial photographs, archival sources, period newspapers, written histories, and fieldwork.

## Key Findings Added

1. Frantzman concludes that 172-195 new Arab settlements were established and permanently inhabited in Palestine between 1871 and 1948.
2. 146 inhabited new Arab settlements were recognized by Mandatory authorities in 1945.
3. 66,940 settlers were living in the new settlements, with 831,486 dunams classified administratively with those villages by the Mandate.
4. A major finding is that most inhabitants of the new settlements were local Arabs from nearby areas.
5. Frantzman states that very few Arab settlers were from outside the borders of Mandatory Palestine.
6. A significant number of new settlements were Bedouin: the dissertation gives both 47 and 52 depending on counting criteria.
7. Egyptian migration associated with Muhammad Ali's Syrian campaign is identified, via Grossman, as the largest 19th-century migration that remained in Palestine.
8. Egyptian settlement locations listed include Baysan, Wadi Araba, Jezreel Valley, Shephelah, coastal plain, and Negev.
9. Algerian-origin settlement leads include Ammuqa, Marus, and Tuleil in the Safed/Huleh region.

## Implication for Current Hypothesis

This source strengthens the case that large-scale internal movement and settlement fixation occurred in Palestine. It weakens any simple claim that most new Arab rural settlement was caused by external Arab immigration. Frantzman's conclusion is explicit: most new settlers were local Arabs from nearby areas, and very few Arab settlers were from outside Mandatory Palestine.

## Methodological Rule

The 66,940 settler figure is a population stock in newly established settlements, not a migration-flow total. It must not be counted as external immigration unless a specific settlement has an origin classification showing settlers came from outside Palestine.
