# Immigration, Refugee Settlement, and Population Growth in Ottoman Palestine, 1830-1914

## Abstract

This paper reassesses the contribution of immigration and refugee settlement to the demographic growth of Ottoman Palestine between 1830 and 1914. It focuses on officially organized settlement of Muslim refugees from the Caucasus and the Balkans, possible Arab migration from neighboring Ottoman provinces and Egypt, and the methodological challenges involved in distinguishing natural increase, internal migration, external immigration, and improved enumeration. The project uses a source-controlled database to distinguish direct evidence from demographic inference.

## Introduction

The demographic history of late Ottoman Palestine remains contested. Standard narratives often emphasize either natural increase and local continuity or, alternatively, substantial late Ottoman immigration. Both approaches can become polemical when estimates are detached from source criticism. This study proposes a documentary reconstruction grounded in Ottoman administrative records, settlement registers, salnames, census tables, and demographic residual analysis.

## Research Question

What role did immigration and refugee settlement play in the population growth of Ottoman Palestine between 1830 and 1914?

## Scope and Definitions

For this paper, Ottoman Palestine is defined primarily as the administrative districts corresponding broadly to Jerusalem, Jaffa, Gaza, Hebron, Acre, Haifa, Safed, Tiberias, Nazareth, and Nablus. The study distinguishes Palestine proper from the broader Ottoman Levant, including Transjordan, Hauran, Golan, Syria, and Lebanon.

## Methodology

The project separates four classes of evidence:

1. Direct migration and settlement records
2. Settlement histories and village foundation evidence
3. Population series from Ottoman and Mandate records
4. Residual demographic estimates

This separation is necessary because excess population growth cannot automatically be treated as external immigration. It may reflect natural increase, improved enumeration, internal rural-to-urban migration, Jewish immigration, Christian migration, seasonal labor, or administrative reclassification.

## Preliminary Findings

The strongest current evidence concerns Circassian settlement in northern Palestine, especially Kfar Kama, Rehaniya, and Gâbe/Gabiya. Reported figures suggest a conservative subtotal of approximately 1,700-1,800 people, with a higher possible estimate around 3,300 if the reported initial population of Gâbe is verified in Ottoman records.

Evidence also indicates that the Ottoman state settled Muslim refugees from Bosnia and possibly other regions in Palestine. However, these streams have not yet been quantified.

Urban growth in Jerusalem, Jaffa, and Haifa suggests possible migration effects, especially in port and commercial centers. However, the external Arab/Muslim component of this growth remains unresolved and must be separated from internal migration and natural increase.

## Preliminary Argument

The available evidence supports the existence of organized Muslim refugee settlement in Ottoman Palestine and makes a moderate immigration scenario plausible. It does not yet support claims that hundreds of thousands of Muslim immigrants entered Palestine during the late Ottoman period. A defensible working range for external Arab/Muslim migration remains tentative and must be tested against primary Ottoman records.

## Evidence Still Needed

The most important missing records are:

- Muhacirin Commission settlement registers
- Syria Vilayet Salnames
- Beirut Vilayet Salnames
- Jerusalem district records
- Ottoman population registers with birthplace or origin markers
- Jaffa and Haifa port records
- Mandate census birthplace tables

## Conclusion

This project is currently in the source-building phase. Its contribution will depend on reconstructing a verifiable chain from Ottoman administrative records to settlement events and population series. Until that work is complete, estimates should remain provisional and explicitly labeled by confidence level.
