# Draft Insert: Frantzman Origin Tables and the External-Migration Question

Frantzman's origin tables complicate his own conclusion that very few Arab settlers came from outside Mandatory Palestine. Table 2.8 classifies the origins of 69 new Arab settlements established between 1871 and 1922. While the largest category is daughter villages, with 19 settlements, the table also identifies 9.5 Bedouin settlements and 8.5 settlements classified as "Foreigners," including Algerians, Egyptians, Circassians, and Bosnians, grouped with Abdul Hamid II's settlers. A further 8 settlements are classified separately as Abdul Hamid II's settlers. These categories are not marginal for the immigration question. Together they represent a substantial share of pre-1922 new settlement formation and require independent origin coding.

Table 3.6, covering the 1922-1931 period, is even more useful because it provides settlement-level origin descriptions. Most new settlements in that period are Bedouin, but the table also identifies Kh. Hawsa as Algerian, Huseiniya as Algerian Bedouin, Nebi Yusha as Lebanese, and Hamma as a settlement associated with railroad, Abdul Hamid II, and effendi investment. The surrounding discussion adds that Muftahira may have been populated by Egyptians and that Hamma was later described as owned by people from Mukheiba in Transjordan.

These findings do not prove mass external immigration. They do show that the external-migration question cannot be dismissed by a single "local settlers" conclusion. The data require a second-stage coding exercise that distinguishes same-village daughter settlement, Palestine-proper internal migration, Bedouin tribal mobility, Southern Levant movement, Ottoman refugee settlement, and explicitly external Arab or Muslim migration.

The paper should use Frantzman against the overly narrow version of Frantzman: his settlement inventory is more favorable to a migration-sensitive interpretation than his summary conclusion suggests.
